Volleyball is two teams, a net, and at most three touches a side. Every rally scores a point. You win the rally when the ball lands on the other team's floor, or when they make a fault. That is enough to join a casual game. The rest of this page covers the rules that come up in the first hour, checked against the FIVB's official rules for 2025 to 2028.

## What is the aim of the game?

Send the ball over the net so that it lands inside the other team's court, and stop them doing the same to you. A ball that lands on a line is in. A rally starts with a serve and ends when the ball hits the floor, goes out, or someone commits a fault.

## How does scoring work?

Every rally is worth a point, whoever served. This is called rally scoring.

| | Official indoor match | A casual game |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Players a side | 6 | Whatever the game is set up for |
| A set is won at | 25 points, two clear | The organiser decides |
| A match is | Best of five sets | Usually as many sets as fit in the time |
| Deciding set | 15 points, two clear | Rarely played |

## What are the three touches?

A team may touch the ball three times before it has to cross the net. The usual pattern is pass, set, attack: one player receives the ball, a second places it near the net, and a third hits it over.

- The same player cannot touch the ball twice in a row.
- A block at the net does not count as one of the three touches indoors.
- The ball must be hit, not caught, held or thrown.
- You may play the ball with any part of your body.

## How does serving work?

The server stands behind the back line and hits the ball over the net with one hand or arm. You get one attempt. A serve that clips the net and still goes over is in play. Underarm serves are legal, and a good way to start.

The team that wins the rally serves next. If that is the team that was receiving, its players rotate first, which is how everyone gets a turn to serve. [How rotation works](/blog/volleyball-rotation-explained) has the detail.

## What are the most common faults?

- **Four touches:** the team uses more than three.
- **Double contact:** one player touches the ball twice in a row.
- **Catch or lift:** the ball is held or carried instead of hit.
- **Net touch:** a player touches the net between the posts while playing the ball.
- **Foot fault:** the server steps on or over the back line before hitting the serve.
- **Out:** the ball lands outside the lines, or hits the ceiling or a post.

## What is different at a pickup game?

There is usually no referee. At our games in Bangkok an organiser welcomes people, splits the teams and keeps time, and players call their own points. Call your own net touches and doubles. If two people disagree, replay the point.

Casual games also relax the fine print. Nobody will whistle a slightly untidy set from a newcomer. The rules that matter are the ones that keep the game fair and safe: three touches, no catching, and stay out of the net.

## Questions people ask

**How many players are on a volleyball team?**
Six a side on court in the indoor game, and two a side in official beach volleyball. Casual games use whatever number suits the court and the turnout. We play six a side indoors and five a side on sand.

**Can you kick the ball in volleyball?**
Yes. The official rules allow the ball to touch any part of the body, so a foot is legal. It is rarely a good idea, but it counts.

**Can the ball hit the net?**
Yes, during a rally and on the serve, as long as it goes over. A player touching the net while playing the ball is the fault, not the ball.

**How long does a game last?**
A set to 25 usually takes twenty to thirty minutes, depending on the rallies. Pickup sessions are booked by time instead: ours last two hours, with teams swapping on and off.
