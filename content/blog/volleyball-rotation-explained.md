In six-a-side volleyball, a team rotates one place clockwise each time it wins the serve back from the other team. The player who arrives at the back-right corner serves. A team that keeps winning points on its own serve does not rotate. That is the whole rule; the rest is knowing where the six places are.

## What are the six positions?

The court is numbered from the server's corner, going anticlockwise.

| Position | Where it is | Row |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Back right | Back row; this player serves |
| 2 | Front right | Front row |
| 3 | Front middle | Front row |
| 4 | Front left | Front row |
| 5 | Back left | Back row |
| 6 | Back middle | Back row |

When the team rotates, everyone moves one place clockwise: the player in 2 drops back to 1 and serves, 1 moves across to 6, 6 to 5, 5 comes forward to 4, 4 to 3, and 3 to 2.

## When does a team rotate?

Only when it wins a rally that the other team served. This is often called a side-out. The sequence is: win the rally, rotate one place, then serve.

If your team is serving and wins the point, nobody moves and the same player serves again. A player keeps serving until the team loses a rally.

## Do you have to stay in your position?

Only at the moment of the serve. Players line up in rotation order for the serve, and once the ball is hit they can move anywhere. That is why you see a setter run to the net as soon as the serve is struck.

The official rule on positions was reworded in the 2025 to 2028 edition, so referees and league players should read the current text. For a casual game, lining up in order and moving after the serve is all you need.

## What can back-row players not do?

The three players in positions 1, 6 and 5 are the back row until the next rotation. They may not block, and they may not attack the ball from above the height of the net while standing in front of the attack line, which is three metres from the net. They can still attack if they jump from behind that line.

This is the rule that makes rotation matter. Your tallest hitter spends three rotations at the net and three at the back.

## How do casual games handle rotation?

Most pickup games keep the simple part and drop the rest. Teams rotate and everyone serves in turn. Nobody specialises, nobody uses a libero, and back-row rules are applied lightly or not at all. If you are new, follow the person in front of you and move one place when your team wins the serve back.

Where more than two teams share a court, there is a second kind of rotation: teams taking turns. At our indoor games in Bangkok three teams of six share the court, so one team rests while two play. The organiser explains on the day how teams swap on and off.

## Questions people ask

**Which way do you rotate in volleyball?**
Clockwise, looking down on your own side of the court. The front-right player moves to the back-right corner and becomes the server.

**Do you rotate in beach volleyball?**
No. Two-a-side beach volleyball has no set positions. The two players only have to keep their service order, taking turns to serve.

**What happens if a team is out of rotation?**
In an official match it is a fault: the other team gets the point and the serve, and the team corrects its order. In a casual game, fix it and carry on.

**Does the libero rotate?**
The libero is a back-row specialist who swaps in for a back-row player and leaves again before reaching the front row. The team's rotation order carries on underneath. Casual games rarely use one.
