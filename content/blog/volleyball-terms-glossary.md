Volleyball has its own vocabulary, and most of it is shouted mid-rally. These are the words you are likely to hear in your first few games, each in one sentence. You do not need to learn them before you play; people will explain as you go.

## What do the basic volleyball terms mean?

| Term | What it means |
| --- | --- |
| Ace | A serve that scores directly, because nobody can return it. |
| Antenna | The thin rod at each side of the net that marks the edge of the space the ball must cross through. |
| Attack | Any hit that sends the ball over the net to score; a spike is the hard version. |
| Attack line | The line three metres from the net on an indoor court; back-row players attack from behind it. |
| Back row | The three players furthest from the net in the current rotation. |
| Block | Players at the net jumping with their hands up to stop an attack as it crosses. |
| Bump | The forearm pass: arms straight and together, ball played off the forearms. |
| Carry | A fault: the ball is held or thrown instead of hit cleanly. Also called a lift. |
| Dig | A defensive pass that keeps a hard-hit ball off the floor. |
| Double | A fault: one player touches the ball twice in a row. |
| Free ball | An easy ball sent over without an attack, giving the other team time to set up. |
| Front row | The three players nearest the net, who may block and attack at the net. |
| Kill | An attack that wins the point outright. |
| Let | A serve that touches the net and still goes over. It is in play. |
| Libero | A back-row defensive specialist in a different-coloured shirt. Casual games rarely use one. |
| Pass | The first touch, usually a bump, that sends the ball to the setter. |
| Rally | The play from the serve until the ball is dead. |
| Rotation | Players moving one place clockwise when their team wins the serve back. |
| Serve | The hit from behind the back line that starts each rally. |
| Set | The second touch: a high, soft ball placed near the net for a teammate to attack. |
| Setter | The player who takes the second touch and decides who attacks. |
| Side-out | Winning a rally the other team served, which gives your team the serve. |
| Spike | A hard overhead attack hit downward into the other court. |
| Tip | A soft attack placed just over the block with the fingertips. |

## Which calls will I hear during a rally?

- **"Mine" or "got it":** I am taking this ball, stay clear. Call it early and loudly.
- **"Yours":** I am leaving this ball for you.
- **"Out":** let it drop, it is landing outside the lines.
- **"Free":** an easy ball is coming over, get ready to attack.
- **"Help":** my pass has gone off target and someone else needs to chase it.

Calling the ball matters more than anything in the table. Most dropped balls in casual games land between two players who each thought the other had it.

## What do 6v6 and 5v5 mean on a game listing?

They are the number of players a side. Six a side is the standard indoor game. ## Questions people ask

**What is the difference between a bump, a set and a spike?**
They are the three touches in order. The bump is the first, off the forearms. The set is the second, placed high near the net. The spike is the third, hit hard over the net.

**What is a side-out in volleyball?**
It is winning a rally when the other team served. Your team gets the point and the serve, and rotates one place.

**What does libero mean?**
A libero is a defensive specialist who plays only in the back row and wears a different-coloured shirt. The libero cannot block or attack above the net.

**What is a pancake in volleyball?**
A last-ditch save: a player slides a flat hand, palm down, onto the floor so the ball bounces up off the back of it.
