Beach volleyball is played by two a side on a smaller sand court, in sets to 21. Indoor volleyball is six a side on a hard court, in sets to 25. The net is the same height in both. Those four facts explain most of what feels different, and the table below has the rest.

## How do beach and indoor volleyball compare?

| | Indoor | Beach |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Players a side | 6, with substitutes | 2, no substitutes |
| Court | 18 m by 9 m | 16 m by 8 m |
| Surface | Hard court | Sand |
| Sets | To 25, best of five | To 21, best of three |
| Deciding set | To 15 | To 15 |
| Changing ends | Between sets | Every 7 points, every 5 in the deciding set |
| Positions | Fixed rotation order | No set positions; service order only |
| A block | Does not count as a touch | Counts as one of the three touches |
| The ball | Smaller, firmer | Slightly larger, softer |
| Net height | 2.43 m men, 2.24 m women | The same |

## Why does two a side change everything?

With six players, each one covers a slice of the court and has a job: setter, hitter, defender. With two, both players do everything. You pass, set, attack and defend on every rally, and there is nowhere to hide.

The official beach rules are also stricter about touch. An open-hand tip over the net is not allowed, and a set has to come out of the hands cleanly. Because a block counts as a touch, a team that blocks has two touches left, not three.

## Is playing on sand harder?

It is harder to move and easier to land. Sand takes the spring out of your jump and the speed out of your first step, so rallies are slower and positioning matters more than power. Falling costs nothing, which is why people dive for balls on sand that they would leave indoors.

Wind and sun are part of the outdoor game, which is the reason teams change ends so often. On an indoor sand court neither applies.

## What is social beach volleyball?

Most casual sand games are not two a side. Groups play three, four or five a side with relaxed rules on hand contact, because it gets more people on court and keeps rallies alive. The FIVB publishes a separate four-a-side version of the beach rules for exactly this kind of play.

Our sand game in Bangkok is five a side on an indoor sand court at Asoke Sports Club, with three teams taking turns. It is social volleyball on sand, not the Olympic format.

## Which should a beginner try first?

Try sand first if you have not played since school. The pace is slower, the landing is soft, and with fewer players a side you touch the ball more often. Try indoor first if you played six a side before and want the faster game with set positions.

Either way, the basics are the same: three touches, no catching, and call your own faults. [Volleyball rules for beginners](/blog/volleyball-rules-for-beginners) covers them, and [this week's games](/games) lists both formats.

## Questions people ask

**Is the net the same height in beach and indoor volleyball?**
Yes. Both use 2.43 metres for men and 2.24 metres for women. [Court size and net height](/blog/volleyball-court-size-and-net-height) has the full measurements.

**Why is a beach court smaller?**
There are two players covering it instead of six, and sand slows everyone down. A beach court is 16 by 8 metres, two metres shorter and one metre narrower than an indoor court.

**Do you wear shoes for beach volleyball?**
Players normally go barefoot on sand. Some wear sand socks when the surface is hot or rough. At an indoor sand court, ask the organiser before your first game.

**Can you play beach volleyball indoors?**
Yes. Indoor sand courts exist so the game can be played in any weather. In Bangkok that means no rain and no midday heat.
